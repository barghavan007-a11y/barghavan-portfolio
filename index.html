Harmonium

A harmonium sitting on a patterned rug in an ornate room slowly comes apart. The wood breaks into chunks and dust, and the music it used to make spills out as glowing gold notes and glitter. I wanted the moment to feel like the instrument is playing its last song as it disappears, and I wanted it to look real rather than CG.

The effect is built in Houdini 22: the harmonium is fractured and driven apart with particles, and the notes, dust and glitter are separate particle systems so each could be timed and shaded on its own. The glitter is real bead geometry so it catches light properly. I rendered the beauty and its passes in Redshift through Solaris, rendered depth and ID mattes in Karma, and put it all together in Nuke with depth haze, glow from the notes lighting the air, bloom, film halation, grain and a light vignette.

Software: Houdini (Solaris, particles), Redshift, Karma, Nuke · Format: 1920 × 1080, 24 fps

Moonlit Shrine

A matte painting of a cave shrine lit only by oil lamps and a crescent moon. The work is built in depth layers, from the wet foreground rock through the seated cow and the sanctum to the open night sky beyond the cave mouth, with atmospheric haze used to separate each plane and hold the scale of the space. Lighting is the core of the piece: the warm pools of lamp flame are kept as the only source of heat in an otherwise cool frame, so the eye travels inward to the central figure without needing any hard compositional lines. Colour, value and edge treatment were balanced across the whole frame to keep every element sitting convincingly in the same light.

I then brought the painting into Nuke and split it into depth layers projected onto cards, so a camera can move through the cave and the foreground, shrine and sky shift against each other like a real space. The end of the video shows the 3D setup.

Georgia Wildfire: building the forest and getting COP Pyro running

The idea

A forest fire in a Georgia pine plantation, seen from a slow drone shot. The fire line creeps through the trees toward a dirt road, a few trees go up in flames, and the smoke builds behind it. I'm doing all the fire in Copernicus Pyro, the new GPU pyro in Houdini, which is the part I'm most excited to learn.

What I looked at

I collected news and drone photos of the Georgia fires and kept noticing the same things: the trees are tall and skinny with all their branches bunched at the top, the ground fire runs in ribbons along the planted rows, single trees "torch" when the fire climbs into the crown, and the roads act as firebreaks. That's what I'm building toward.

What I got done today

The forest. Everything is procedural. The terrain is a heightfield, and it's almost flat because south Georgia really is that flat. I wrote a small pine tree generator in VEX that builds the trunk, the bare lower stubs, the branches at the top and the needle clumps, and gives me four different trees. Those get scattered in planted rows (about 4,000 trees), with a sand road cut through the middle and a couple of clear-cut patches like in the photos.

The fire line. Before any simulation, I needed to know where the fire is on every frame. I built an animated "fire front" on the ground that moves toward the camera with some noise so it breaks into fingers instead of a straight line. Anything the front passes turns black, a few embers stay glowing, and the road stops it, except for a couple of small spot fires that jump across a few seconds in. About one in eight trees catch fire in the crown when the front reaches them.

The pyro. I started from SideFX's built-in "Pyro Configure Fire" setup in Copernicus and swapped its test source for my own. The burning parts of the fire line and the torching trees get turned into density, temperature, flame and velocity volumes, and those feed the COP Pyro solver. I set it up at real-world scale (half-metre voxels) and added a light wind blowing toward the camera. It sims fast on the GPU, which makes it easy to iterate.

What went wrong

  • Houdini crashed a few times today. I tracked it down to Python scripts poking at the COP Pyro nodes. Building the setup through the normal UI works fine, so I'm sticking with that.
  • The fire looks too puffy right now, like a row of little mushroom clouds. Each source point makes its own blob, so the line doesn't read as one continuous fire yet.

Next steps

  • Make the flame band wider so the fire reads as one connected line.
  • Drop the voxel size to 0.25 m for more detail once the look is right.
  • Add more turbulence and let the smoke hang around longer, so it builds into a proper column like the reference.
  • Add embers with POPs, and a burnt bark shader for the trees.
  • Light it and render to EXR.
